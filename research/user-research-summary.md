## User Research Summary

### 1. Introduction

User research was conducted to understand the experiences and challenges students face when dealing with lost and found items on campus. The research focused on how students currently report lost items, search for missing belongings, return found items, and claim items that they believe belong to them.

A total of five university students participated in the research. The participants represented the target users of the proposed Student Lost and Found Mobile Application.

The information collected will be used to guide the application's information architecture, user flows, mobile interface design, and interactive Balsamiq prototype

---

### 2. Research Objectives

The user research aimed to:

-	Understand students' experiences with lost and found items.
-	Identify the methods students currently use to find lost belongings.
-	Identify challenges associated with reporting and searching for lost items.
-	Determine the information students would provide when reporting lost or found items.
-	Identify useful search and filtering options.
-	Understand students' concerns about claiming items.
-	Identify usability requirements for a mobile Lost and Found application.

### 4. Key Research Findings

#### 4.1 Experience with Lost and Found Items

**Finding:**

The interviews showed that all five participants had experienced situations involving lost or found belongings on campus. Some participants had personally lost items, while others had found items belonging to other students. A student ID was identified as one of the items that had been lost, while other personal belongings were also mentioned. Participants commonly relied on friends, nearby locations, and campus communication channels when trying to recover or return items.

**Design Implication:**

The application should make it easy for students to report both lost and found items and provide relevant details such as the item category, location, date, description, and image.

#### 4.2 Current Methods of Finding Lost Items

**Finding:**

Participants reported using informal methods to search for lost items. These included asking friends, checking the areas where the item was last seen, asking security personnel, and using student communication channels such as WhatsApp groups. One participant who found an item used a WhatsApp group to try to locate its owner. These methods can make it difficult to reach the correct person and may result in information being scattered across different platforms.

**Design Implication:**

The application should provide a centralized platform where students can report and search for lost and found items instead of relying on several disconnected methods.

#### 4.3 Problems Experienced by Users

**Finding:**

The main problems identified were the lack of a centralized system for lost and found items and difficulty knowing where or how to formally report a missing item. Participants also relied heavily on friends and communication groups, which may not always reach the person who has found the item. The lack of an organized search process was another concern.

**Design implication:**

The application should provide clear navigation, organized information, search and filtering tools, and a transparent reporting and claiming process.

#### 4.4 Important Item Information

**Finding:**

Participants indicated that useful information should be provided when reporting a lost or found item. This includes the item name, category, location where it was lost or found, date, description, color, and a photograph where possible. Identifying features would also be useful when determining whether an item belongs to a particular student.

**Design implication:**

The report forms should contain clearly labeled fields and allow users to provide sufficient information to help identify an item.

#### 4.5 Search and Filtering

**Finding:**

Participants preferred a more organized way of searching for lost and found items rather than relying on friends or communication groups. Useful search options identified from the interviews include searching by item name or keyword and filtering results according to category, location, date, and other identifying characteristics such as color.

**Design implication:**

The application should provide a simple search interface with relevant filters to help students quickly narrow down the list of lost and found items.

#### 4.6 Claiming Items

**Finding:**

Participants expressed the need for a way to verify ownership before an item is handed over to a person claiming it. There was concern that someone could falsely claim an item if there were no verification measures. Participants therefore preferred a claiming process that requires the claimant to provide information that can be compared with the details of the reported item.

**Design implication:**

The claim process should include verification questions or identifying information to reduce false claims. The user should also be able to track the status of their claim.

#### 4.7 Feedback and Notifications

**Finding:**

Participants indicated that they would benefit from receiving updates after reporting a lost or found item and after submitting a claim. They would also like to know whether their report has been received and whether their claim is still being processed or has been resolved.

**Design implication:**

The application should provide clear feedback after actions such as submitting a report or claim. Users should also be able to see whether a report or claim is pending, approved, rejected, or resolved.

### 5. Summary of User Needs

Based on the research findings, the Student Lost and Found Mobile Application should provide the following:


| User Need | Proposed Design Solution |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| Report lost items easily | Simple lost-item form |
| Report found items | Simple found-item form |
| Provide enough item information | Description, category, location, date and image |
| Find items quickly | Search functionality |
| Narrow search results | Filters |
| Confirm ownership | Verification during claiming |
| Know claim progress | Claim tracking |
| Receive updates | Notifications |
| Use the application comfortably | Simple, touch-friendly mobile interface |

---

### 6. HCI Design Implications

The research findings will directly influence the design of the Balsamiq prototype.  
---
#### Simple Mobile Forms

The lost and found reporting forms should contain only necessary information and clearly indicate required fields

#### Search Interaction

Search and filtering should be easy to locate and use, allowing students to quickly find relevant items.

#### Feedback

The application should provide immediate feedback after important actions such as submitting a report or claim.

#### Error Prevention

The design should prevent common errors by using required fields, clear instructions, confirmation before submission, and appropriate validation.

#### Touch-Friendly Interaction

Buttons, input fields, navigation controls, and other interactive elements should be large enough and appropriately spaced for smartphone use.

#### Clear Navigation

Users should be able to move between major sections such as Home, Search, Report Item, My Reports, and My Claims without confusion.

---

### 7. Research Conclusion

The user research provided useful information about the needs and challenges of students when dealing with lost and found items on campus. The research showed that students currently rely heavily on informal methods such as asking friends, checking locations, and using communication groups.

The findings indicate a need for a centralized platform that simplifies reporting, searching, and claiming lost and found items. Important features identified include item reporting forms, image uploads, search and filtering, ownership verification, claim tracking, and notifications.

These findings will guide the development of the application's information architecture, user flows, mobile forms, search functionality, claiming process, and feedback mechanisms.

The identified user needs will be incorporated into the Balsamiq prototype. After the initial prototype is completed, usability testing will be conducted with at least five users. The feedback obtained during testing will be used to identify usability problems and improve the final prototype.
